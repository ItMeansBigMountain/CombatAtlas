# BurnoutBoyz vehicle and maintenance data stack research

Task: choose a launch-safe source stack for VIN identity, recalls, fuel/economy attributes, OEM maintenance intervals, owner-manual workflows, connected-car odometer, service bulletins, labor/parts/fluids, and credible commercial providers.

## Executive recommendation

Launch with a free official foundation and no OEM schedule claims: NHTSA vPIC for VIN/year/make/model/configuration identity, NHTSA Recalls for active safety recall display, FuelEconomy.gov for EPA vehicle variants and fuel/emissions attributes, and user-entered mileage/service history.[1][2][3] Do not call generic intervals OEM data; generic rules may exist only as clearly labeled non-OEM guidance.

For true OEM maintenance schedules, license a commercial schedule provider before showing vehicle-specific intervals. Vehicle Databases is the most launch-ready schedule API to trial first because it publicly claims VIN/YMM lookup, trim resolution, structured JSON, U.S. 1983-present coverage, mileage intervals through 200,000+ miles, sandbox/batch support, and a free trial.[7] DataOne is the best enterprise shortlist if BurnoutBoyz also needs high-quality VIN/trim/build/spec/service datasets, but pricing and redistribution terms require sales review.[13][16]

For repair-shop depth, labor, parts, fluids, and procedural context, Mitchell 1 ProDemand and ALLDATA are credible licensed ecosystems, but they are likely UI/subscription/integration partnerships rather than a simple consumer-display data feed; treat them as paid partner lanes after MVP validation.[10][11][24]

For connected-car enhancements, Smartcar should be optional only. It can provide consented live vehicle signals such as odometer/VIN/vehicle attributes across compatible connected vehicles, but coverage varies by brand, model, year, region, trim, packages, firmware, and active subscriptions, so manual odometer entry remains required.[18][19][21]

## Free-vs-paid launch plan

Phase 0 / free launch can safely include VIN decode, manual vehicle confirmation, manual mileage, service-history entry, FuelEconomy attributes, and NHTSA recall checks.[1][2][3] The UI must say “OEM maintenance schedule unavailable until licensed data is connected” unless the vehicle has a licensed schedule result or user-supplied/manual-entered source.

Phase 1 / paid schedule beta should trial Vehicle Databases first because it is explicitly packaged as a maintenance schedule API with JSON output, VIN/YMM lookup, trim resolution, sandbox/batch support, and free trial.[7] Required vendor questions before use: consumer display rights, redistribution/caching, attribution, rate limits, update cadence, coverage exceptions, time-based intervals, severe-service schedules, Canadian/California/regional variants, warranty/recall disclaimers, cancellation/data purge terms, and whether schedule strings may be stored as normalized tasks.

Phase 2 / enterprise data should compare DataOne, MOTOR, Mitchell 1, and ALLDATA. DataOne is strongest for vehicle identity/spec/build/service-data breadth with API/SFTP options; Mitchell and ALLDATA are strongest for shop-grade repair/labor/fluid/maintenance context but may require user subscriptions or UI intents rather than raw data feeds.[13][15][16][10][11][24]

## Product guardrails

1. Every maintenance item must carry `source_type`, `provider`, `license_id`, `source_version`, `retrieved_at`, `vehicle_match_confidence`, and `normal_or_severe_use_assumption`.
2. VIN decode confidence is not schedule confidence. vPIC can identify many attributes, but exact trim/equipment is not guaranteed; DataOne notes trim/transmission are not consistently encoded in VIN and advanced decoders use extra data.[16]
3. If no licensed OEM schedule exists, the app may show only user history, recall status, manual links, and clearly labeled general education.
4. Do not scrape owner manuals, ALLDATA, Mitchell, OEM portals, or bulletin PDFs into a reusable database unless the license explicitly permits it.
5. Connected-car data is sensitive personal data. Keep Smartcar optional, use least-privilege permissions such as odometer/VIN only when needed, and preserve manual entry as the default path.[18][19]

## Vendor diligence checklist

- Legal: consumer app display rights, cached normalized-task storage, screenshot/export rights, attribution, white-label restrictions, data deletion obligations, OEM sublicensing limits.
- Coverage: make/model/year/trim, powertrain, region, severe-use, time-based intervals, EV/hybrid high-voltage items, recalls, TSB/manufacturer communications, fluids, labor, parts, warranty applicability.
- Technical: endpoint shapes, IDs, batch limits, rate limits, webhooks/SFTP cadence, versioning/diffs, error taxonomy, sample data for 20 representative vehicles.
- Commercial: trial length, startup pricing, minimums, overage, cancellation, SLA/support, production approval timeline.
- Safety: disclaimers, source provenance, date/version locking, human-edit workflow, “unknown” state instead of guessed service completion.
